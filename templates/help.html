Quick start guide

Your first transmission in a few minutes. Follow the steps in order — the home page is laid out the same way (cards 1, 2 and 3).

✅ Before you start

  • The Pluto is connected to the Jetson with its USB cable, and its lights are on.
  • The Pluto runs the latest PlutoDVB2 firmware by Evariste (F5OEO) — the custom firmware that turns it into a DATV transmitter. Download: github.com/F5OEO/plutosdr-fw/releases.
  • The antenna / dish and power amplifier are connected. Never transmit into an open connector.
  • You have a receiver to watch your own signal (for example MiniTioune or OpenTuner, with an LNB on the same dish).
  • You hold an amateur radio licence that allows transmitting to QO-100, and your callsign is set on the Setup page.
1

Choose what to send

Card 1 on the home page. Click one of the three buttons:

  • Testcard — a still test picture with a little melody. Use the ‹ › arrows on the picture to choose one. The last one, “SBB Clock (live)”, is a live station clock with test tones.
  • Camera — live picture and sound from the USB camera. Choose the camera and the microphone below the preview.
  • Video — a prepared video file. New videos are added in the Video Library.
💡 First time? Use the Testcard. It is the easiest to check on your receiver.
2

Choose where to send it

Card 2 shows the BATC wideband spectrum: what is on the satellite right now. Below it is a row of small bars — each bar is one channel.

  • Green = free. Red = somebody is using it.
  • Click a green bar. Its frequency appears in the Freq. box. Never choose a red one.
  • SR (symbol rate): 333 is narrower and easier to receive. 500 gives a better picture but needs a stronger signal.
  • FEC: 3/4 gives a better picture, 2/3 is more robust when reception is difficult.
💡 Not sure? Keep SR 333 and FEC 3/4 (the defaults).
3

Go on air

  1. TX power starts at 0%. Leave it low for now.
  2. Press ▶ Start stream. After a few seconds the button turns red and says ■ Stop stream.
  3. Look at the Local Pluto RX spectrum (right). A flat-topped hump means your Pluto is transmitting.
  4. Only if you use the optional PA relay (see below): wait until card 3 shows PA: ready, then press ⚡ Engage PA relay. If the signal becomes unstable, the relay switches off again by itself.
  5. Raise TX power slowly while you watch your signal appear in the BATC spectrum.
  6. Check your receiver: you should see your picture and hear the sound.
  7. When you are done, press ■ Stop stream. TX power goes back to 0% (and the PA relay switches off) by itself.
⚠️ Your signal must always stay lower than the beacon — the big block on the left of the BATC spectrum. If you are as strong as the beacon, reduce TX power immediately.

⚡ The PA relay (optional)

You don't need it — everything works without. It is a safety switch, for example for the first stage of your power amplifier (driver / pre-amp):

💡 Why? The Pluto can send short full-power spikes while it starts up or is reconfigured — whatever the power setting. These spikes can destroy a power amplifier or pre-amp. The relay keeps the PA switched off until the Pluto is running and its signal is clean and stable.
  • It switches on only when you press Engage PA relay, and only while the Pluto's signal is clean and stable.
  • It switches off by itself when you press Stop, when the transmission ends or crashes, or when the signal becomes unstable.
  • Wiring: physical pin 18 of the Jetson's 40-pin header (ground e.g. on pin 20). Engaged = 3.3 V, off = 0 V.
⚠️ Pin 18 means the physical pin number on the header — not “GPIO18” (on Raspberry Pi naming that is a different pin). The pin can only deliver a few milliamps: use a relay module with a 3.3 V logic input (transistor or optocoupler). Connecting a bare relay coil directly can damage the Jetson.

🎬 Adding your own videos

  1. A USB stick must be plugged into the Jetson and set up once on the Setup page.
  2. Open the Video Library, choose a file, give it a name and press Upload & prepare video.
  3. The Jetson now prepares the video. This takes time — roughly as long as the video itself (a 1½-hour film ≈ 80 minutes).
  4. When it says Ready for DATV, the video appears under Video on the home page.
⚠️ While a video is being prepared: do not unplug the USB stick, do not restart the Jetson or the app. The preparation would be lost and has to start again.

⚙️ The Setup page

  • Callsign — your callsign, shown in the picture and sent to the Pluto. Changing it restarts the Pluto.
  • Top/bottom banner & marquee — the title bar, the bottom bar (callsign, clock, frequency) and the scrolling text shown with Camera and Video.
  • Add prepared videos via USB — choose which USB stick holds your videos.
  • Restart app — if something is stuck. Never while a video is being prepared.

🛠️ Something is not working?

“Select a frequency first”
Click a green bar under the BATC spectrum before pressing Start.
The BATC spectrum is empty or says DISCONNECTED
It comes from the internet. Check the Jetson's network connection.
The RX spectrum still says NO SIGNAL after Start
Check the Pluto's USB cable, then press Stop and Start again.
“PA: not ready” does not change (PA relay users)
The signal is not stable yet. Wait a few seconds. If nothing changes, press Stop and Start again.
The picture stutters on my receiver
First try another receiver program or player. The Jetson sends a steady stream.
My video is not in the list
It is still being prepared (see the Video Library), or the USB stick is not plugged in.
A page looks wrong after an update
Reload the page (F5).

📡 Good manners on QO-100

  • Only use free (green) channels.
  • Never be stronger than the beacon.
  • Keep test transmissions short.
  • Your callsign is shown in the picture — make sure it is set correctly on the Setup page.
